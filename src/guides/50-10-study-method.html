Some days, 25 minutes is not enough. You just get into a chapter, and the timer rings. The 50/10 study method gives you a longer, calmer block: 50 minutes of focus, then a 10-minute break. Simple to remember, easy to repeat.

What is the 50/10 study method?

It is a way to split your study time into blocks. You focus on one task for 50 minutes. Then you rest for 10 minutes. Then you do it again.

It is like a longer cousin of the Pomodoro Technique (25 minutes of focus + 5 minutes of rest). Fifty minutes is long enough to really get into a topic. Ten minutes is long enough to rest your mind, but short enough that you come back.

How to do it, step by step

  1. Pick one task. Not "study biology". Something clear, like "make notes on chapter 4" or "solve 10 practice questions".
  2. Put your phone away. Another room is best. If that is hard, turn it face down and silent.
  3. Start a 50-minute timer and work only on that task. If a thought pops up ("I must reply to that message"), write it on a "later" list and keep going.
  4. Stop when the timer rings, even in the middle of a sentence. Stand up and take your 10-minute break.
  5. Repeat. After 2 or 3 blocks, take a longer break of 20 to 30 minutes.

Tip: stopping in the middle of a sentence is a small trick. It makes it much easier to start again after the break, because you know exactly where you were.

What to do in your 10-minute break

The break is not "lost time". It is part of the method. A good break rests the part of your brain you were using. A bad break fills it up with new things, so you come back still tired.

Great breaks ✅Breaks that steal your focus ❌
Stand up, stretch, walk aroundScrolling social media
Drink water, have a small snackStarting a new video or episode
Look out of a window, get some fresh airGames ("just one round")
A short chat with someone at homeLong message threads that pull you in

A good test: can you stop it after 10 minutes without feeling cut off? If not, it is probably a focus-stealer.

50/10 vs Pomodoro (25/5)

Pomodoro (25/5)50/10
Focus time25 minutes50 minutes
Break5 minutes10 minutes
Best forHard-to-start days, short tasks, flash cardsLong reading, writing, maths and science problems
Feels likeQuick wins, many small stepsFewer, deeper blocks

A good path: start with 25/5. When 25 minutes starts to feel easy, move up to 50/10.

Is 50/10 right for you?

Try it if: the Pomodoro bell keeps cutting you off in the middle of good work, or you study long subjects like history, law, medicine or engineering.

Try something gentler if: 50 minutes feels impossible today. That is completely normal. Start with 25 minutes, or even just 2 minutes to get going. Starting is the hardest part.

Common mistakes

  • The break keeps growing. 10 minutes becomes 40. Use a timer for the break too.
  • Doing three things in one block. One block, one task.
  • Skipping every break "because I'm in the zone". Once is fine. Every time, and you will fade later in the day.
  • Phone on the desk. Even a silent phone pulls your attention. Put it away.
